# Solar System (sample notes)

The Sun contains about 99.86 percent of the total mass of the solar system. Its energy comes from nuclear fusion, where hydrogen nuclei fuse into helium in the core.

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. It has almost no atmosphere, so temperatures swing from very hot in the day to extremely cold at night.

Venus is the hottest planet, not Mercury, because its thick carbon dioxide atmosphere traps heat through a runaway greenhouse effect. It also rotates backwards compared to most planets.

Jupiter is the largest planet. Its Great Red Spot is a giant storm that has been observed for more than 300 years. Jupiter has dozens of moons, including the four large Galilean moons: Io, Europa, Ganymede and Callisto.

Saturn is best known for its rings, made mostly of ice particles and rock. Saturn is the least dense planet; its average density is lower than water.
